Spot the pattern

Patterns are hiding in plain sight

Staircase

Each flight rises by the same number of steps as the one before.

rule: +1 step, every flight

Honeycomb

Bees add a fixed number of new cells with every ring they build.

rule: +6 cells, per ring

Canteen tables

Push tables together in a row and the seats grow by a constant amount.

rule: +2 seats, per table

Once you see the rule, you can predict what comes next — even the 100th step.

Term

Each individual number in a sequence. 5, 8, 11 and 14 are all terms.

Position

Where a term sits in the sequence — 1st, 2nd, 3rd, and so on.

Tn

The term in position n. It's the formula that gives you any term you ask for.

Sequence: 5, 9, 13, 17 — what is the common difference, d?

Subtract the 1st term from the 2nd term: 9 − 5 = ?
Step 1: 9 − 5 = 4 Step 2: 13 − 9 = 4 → same difference, so it's constant Step 3: the common difference d = 4
✓Practice 2

Sequence: 30, 25, 20, 15 — what is the common difference, d?

The terms are getting smaller. Subtract: 25 − 30 = ?
Step 1: 25 − 30 = −5 Step 2: 20 − 25 = −5 → same difference Step 3: d = −5 (negative, because the terms decrease)
✨
Takeaway

Every sequence hides a rule — subtract consecutive terms to find the common difference, d. A positive d means the sequence grows; a negative d means it shrinks.

Why bother finding a formula?

😩 The slow way

"What's the 50th term of 5, 8, 11, 14, …?"  You'd have to write out all 50 terms, one by one, just to answer one question.

⚡ The smart way

Turn the sequence into a formula, Tn. Then finding the 50th term — or the 500th — takes one substitution. No counting.

Takeaway

Every linear sequence can be written as Tn = dn + c. Find d from the common difference, then substitute n=1 to solve for c. That formula works for any position, not just the ones you were given.

✓Practice 9

Why is it useful to find the formula for Tn, instead of just listing terms one by one?

Think about how long it would take to list 100 terms by hand, one at a time.
Listing every term to find, say, the 100th term would take forever. The formula Tn = dn + c lets us jump straight to any term by substituting its position, n. That's the real power of finding the nth term.
✨
Takeaway

Number sequences aren't just abstract maths — the same dn + c method models real patterns like seating rows, savings, and growth, wherever something changes by a constant amount.
